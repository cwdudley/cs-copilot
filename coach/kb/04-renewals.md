# Renewals

## Risk Classification

**Red**
- Health in red band
- P1 tickets open
- No exec contact in 45+ days (Enterprise)
- Tier 3 churn signals present

**Yellow**
- Yellow health band
- Declining trends
- NPS at or below 6
- Three or more open support tickets
- Renewal conversation stalled

**Green**
- No red or yellow signals
- Health stable or improving
- Renewal conversation active

## Decision posture

A false positive costs an extra call. A false negative costs a surprise churn.
When in doubt, escalate earlier.

---

## Key metrics

**GRR — Gross Revenue Retention.** Excludes expansion; measures pure retention.
GRR below target is a structural health problem, not a sales problem. It means
you are losing customers or seats you already had.

**NRR — Net Revenue Retention.** Includes expansion, but **capped at qualified
pipeline only**. Unqualified expansion signals are tagged as early signals and
excluded from NRR. Counting unqualified signals inflates NRR and produces
forecasts that miss.

---

## Renewal language rule

Never use language implying a revenue commitment without finance review. Frame
forecasts as probability-weighted ranges with explicit assumptions, not
commitments.

- Not: "That account will renew at 120K."
- Instead: "Base case is flat at 100K, with an upside case to 120K if the
  second team onboards before Q3 — that assumes their platform hire starts on
  schedule."

---

## Qualifying a renewal (model note)

Renewals need qualification, not just monitoring. Run MEDDPICC — see the
MEDDPICC document for full detail.

Elements that are easy to skip on renewals:

1. **Paper Process** — MEDDICC names this as where renewals slip. Legal,
   procurement, and security review take time nobody scheduled. Start 90 days out
   for enterprise.
2. **Economic Buyer** — the champion relationship feels sufficient. It is not.
   Budget owners change.
3. **Competition** — often budget competition, not product competition. Ask
   what else competes for the same line item.

**Model heuristic, not a MEDDICC rule:** treat three or more Unknowns inside the
renewal window as a sign the forecast is not credible, and say so plainly rather
than probability-weighting a guess.

---

## Re-diagnosing before renewal (model note)

If anything material changed — champion exit, reorg, escalation, budget-owner
change — re-run SPICED from scratch. The Situation captured at last renewal
belongs to a company that may no longer exist in that form.

The renewal date is *your* Critical Event, not theirs. Find out what actually
forces a decision on their side.
