# SPICED — Diagnostic Framework

SPICED is a customer-centric diagnostic framework (Winning by Design). In account
management it is **not** a one-time discovery checklist. It is kept current across
every stage — onboarding, mid-term, renewal, expansion — and actively re-run when
something changes, because the answers change as the account changes.

The five elements:

| Element | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **S**ituation | What is actually true about this account right now? |
| **P**ain | What is broken, and who feels it? |
| **I**mpact | What does fixing it move, in numbers? |
| **C**ritical Event | What forces a decision by a date? |
| **D**ecision | Who decides, how, and against what criteria? |

---

## S — Situation

The current state. Firmographics, tech stack, team structure, workflow, and —
most importantly — **what changed recently**.

**Good looks like:** You can describe how the customer works today without
guessing, and you know what is different from 90 days ago.

**Failure mode:** Relying on the Situation captured at handoff. Situation goes
stale quickly: reorgs, budget cycles, new tooling, and headcount changes
invalidate it.

**AM application:** Re-establish Situation at the start of every QBR. If you
cannot name one thing that changed since last quarter, you have not done
discovery — you have done a status update.

---

## P — Pain

The problem and its consequences. Distinguish the **symptom** from the
**underlying cause**, and always identify *who personally feels it*.

**Good looks like:** Pain is attributed to a named person with a role and a
stake. "The support team is frustrated" is not pain. "Dana, the support lead, is
staffing weekend coverage she did not budget for" is pain.

**Failure mode:** Accepting the first answer. The first pain a customer names is
usually the symptom they are most tired of, not the one that costs the most.

**AM application:** Pain in an existing account is often *operational drag*
rather than a missing feature. Look for workarounds — spreadsheets, manual
handoffs, shadow processes. Those are pain with a cost attached.

---

## I — Impact

The quantified business outcome of resolving the Pain. This requires a number.

**Good looks like:** A figure the customer stated themselves, in their units —
hours, dollars, headcount, percentage points, days of cycle time.

**Failure mode:** You supply the number instead of the customer. An Impact you
calculated is a proposal. An Impact they calculated is evidence.

**AM application:** This is where SPICED connects to the Two-Layer Outcome Model.
Impact you assert from market-level data is Layer 1. Impact the customer has
measured in their own environment is Layer 2. Only Layer 2 survives a renewal
conversation with procurement.

> Pain without Impact is a complaint. Impact without a number is an assertion.

---

## C — Critical Event

The deadline or forcing function that makes inaction costly. A date, and a
consequence attached to that date.

**Good looks like:** "Our current vendor contract lapses March 31 and legal will
not grant an extension twice." Both a date and a consequence.

**Failure mode:** Treating the renewal date as the Critical Event. The renewal
date is *your* deadline, not theirs. It creates urgency for you and none for
them.

**AM application:** Expansion conversations stall indefinitely without a Critical
Event. If you cannot name one, the honest forecast is "no date," not "next
quarter." A missing Critical Event is a common reason expansion slips.

---

## D — Decision

The decision process, the criteria, and the people. Overlaps deliberately with
MEDDPICC, which goes deeper on the mechanics.

**Good looks like:** You can name who signs, who can veto, what criteria they
will evaluate against, and what the sequence of approvals is.

**Failure mode:** Assuming renewals do not have a decision process. Auto-renew
clauses hide the process right up until the year someone decides to review it.

**AM application:** When a renewal is above a threshold or the account has had an
escalation, assume the decision process is *more* rigorous than last cycle, not
less. Confirm it rather than inheriting last year's map.

---

## Using SPICED in an account review

Review the five and say out loud where the gaps are. Winning by Design notes that
Impact and Critical Events surface throughout a conversation rather than in a
fixed sequence. A SPICED with three
strong elements and two unknowns is a normal, healthy diagnostic result — the
value is in naming the unknowns, not in pretending they are filled.

**Triggers to re-run SPICED from scratch:**

- Champion departure — the old SPICED belonged to the old champion
- Reorg or budget-owner change
- Escalation or a P1 incident
- Any renewal above your enterprise threshold
- Expansion signal crossing into qualification

## Common coaching corrections

- **"They love us"** — that is sentiment, not Situation. What changed?
- **"They need better reporting"** — symptom. What does the absence of it cost?
- **"It'll save them a ton of time"** — whose estimate? Get theirs.
- **"They want to do it this year"** — that is an intention, not a Critical Event.
- **"Renewal is automatic"** — until it is reviewed. Confirm the process.
