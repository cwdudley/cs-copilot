# MEDDPICC — Qualification Framework

MEDDPICC qualifies whether a revenue event is **real and winnable**. In account
management it applies to renewals and expansions, not just new logos — and the
common mistake is assuming existing accounts do not need qualifying.

They do. A renewal you have not qualified is a forecast built on habit.

|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics |
| **E** | Economic Buyer |
| **D** | Decision Criteria |
| **D** | Decision Process |

## M — Metrics

The quantified economic impact, expressed as a number the customer will defend
internally without you in the room.

**Test:** Could your champion repeat this number to their CFO and stand behind
it?

**AM application:** For renewals, Metrics are *retrospective* — what the account
has actually realized. This is Layer 2 evidence from the Two-Layer Outcome Model.
For expansion, Metrics are prospective and need the customer's own model.

**Weak:** "They've seen great efficiency gains."
**Strong:** "They measured 11 hours a week saved across 14 analysts, which their
ops director costed at roughly $180K annually."

---

## E — Economic Buyer

The person with discretionary budget authority for this spend. Not the champion.
Not the daily user. Not necessarily the signer.

**Test:** Have you personally met them within the current cycle?

**AM application:** This is the most commonly skipped element in CS, because the
champion relationship feels sufficient. It is not. Economic Buyers change more
often than champions do, and a budget owner you have never spoken to has no
relationship with you to weigh against a cheaper alternative.

Your **expansion qualification gate** — a conversation with the Economic Buyer
plus a formal CRM opportunity — is exactly MEDDPICC's E and Decision Process. An
expansion signal that clears neither is a lead, not pipeline.

---

## D — Decision Criteria

The technical, business, and relationship criteria the account will evaluate
against.

**Test:** Can you list them in the customer's words and in their priority order?

**AM application:** Renewal criteria are frequently *different* from the original
purchase criteria. They bought on capability; they renew on reliability, support
responsiveness, and realized value. Criteria that were decisive at purchase may
be irrelevant now.

---

## D — Decision Process

The actual sequence of steps, reviews, and approvals — who is involved at each
stage and in what order.

**Test:** Can you draw it as a sequence with names and rough dates?

**AM application:** Ask directly: *"Walk me through what happens between now and
signature."* If the answer is vague, the date in your forecast is a guess.

---

## P — Paper Process

Legal, procurement, security review, vendor onboarding, MSA redlines, insurance
certificates. The administrative path from "yes" to countersigned.

**Test:** Do you know how long it took last time, and what it required?

**AM application:** **This is where renewals slip past the date most often**, and
it is almost entirely preventable. The commercial decision is made on time and
the paperwork takes six weeks nobody scheduled.

Start Paper Process in parallel with the commercial conversation, not after it.
For enterprise renewals, ask about procurement timelines 90 days out.

---

## I — Identify Pain

The compelling business pain that justifies spending. Connects directly to
SPICED's P and I.

**Test:** Is the pain severe enough that doing nothing has a visible cost?

**AM application:** For renewals, the operative pain is often the **cost of
switching or losing** the capability rather than a new problem. That is
legitimate — but only if the customer articulates it. If you are the only one
describing the cost of leaving, you have not identified pain, you have made an
argument.

---

## C — Champion

Someone with internal influence who sells on your behalf when you are not in the
room, and who has a personal win tied to the outcome.

**Three tests — all required:**

1. **Power** — do others listen to them?
2. **Advocacy** — do they actively sell internally, unprompted?
3. **Personal win** — what do *they* get if this succeeds?

**AM application:** Champions are routinely assumed rather than tested. A
friendly daily user who likes you is a **coach**, not a champion. Coaches give
you information; champions spend political capital.

Test a champion by asking them to do something that costs them a little — arrange
an Economic Buyer introduction, pull a stakeholder into a review, surface an
internal objection. A champion does it. A coach deflects.

**Champion departure is a Tier 2 churn signal and invalidates this element
entirely.** Re-qualify from scratch; do not inherit.

---

## C — Competition

Every alternative, including the two that win most often in existing accounts:

- **Do nothing / status quo** — the most common competitor at renewal
- **Build in-house** — especially after a headcount increase or platform hire
- **Consolidation into an incumbent suite** — a vendor they already pay
- **A direct competitor** — usually the least likely at renewal, despite getting
  the most attention

**AM application:** In renewals, competition is usually *budget* competition, not
product competition. You are not losing to a rival tool; you are losing to a
different line item. Ask what else is competing for the same budget.

---

## Scoring a renewal or expansion

Score each element **Known / Partial / Unknown**. Do not average them into a
single number — the pattern matters more than the score.

**Structural risk patterns:**

- Economic Buyer unknown + renewal inside 90 days → escalate now
- Champion unknown or untested + high ACV → single-threaded, treat as at-risk
- Paper Process unknown + enterprise account → the date will slip
- Metrics unknown → you cannot defend price, and discounting becomes the only lever
- Competition unknown → you will find out during negotiation, at the worst moment

**Forecast discipline:** Three or more Unknowns on a deal inside the renewal
window means the forecast is not credible. Say so plainly rather than
probability-weighting a guess.

---

## Common coaching corrections

- **"Our champion has it handled"** — has the champion been tested, or just liked?
- **"It's a formality, they always renew"** — name the Economic Buyer and the
  Paper Process, then say that again.
- **"No competition, they love the product"** — status quo and in-house build are
  always on the ballot.
- **"Procurement is just a rubber stamp"** — how long did it take last cycle?
- **"They know the value"** — can they state the number without you?
