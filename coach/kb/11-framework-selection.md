# Choosing a Framework

Three frameworks, three different jobs. Reaching for the wrong one is a common
reason coaching advice feels generic.

| Framework | Job | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **SPICED** | Diagnosis | What is true about this account? |
| **MEDDPICC** | Qualification | Is this revenue event real and winnable? |
| **TARO** | Execution | What play do I run, and what does success look like? |

They are complementary, not competing. SPICED is the running state of the
account, actively invoked when understanding a problem, change, opportunity,
impact, urgency or decision. Account planning decides where to pursue.
MEDDPICC qualifies and executes once a credible renewal or expansion
opportunity exists. Do not force every interaction through any of them.

---

## Routing by what the person asks

**Reach for SPICED when:**
- They are describing a situation and do not yet know what is wrong
- Something changed — reorg, champion exit, usage drop, new stakeholder
- Preparing for a QBR or a discovery call
- The account "feels off" but the health score is green
- They are about to propose a solution before naming the problem

**Reach for MEDDPICC when:**
- A renewal or expansion is being forecast
- They are assessing whether a deal is real
- Something stalled and nobody knows why
- Deciding whether to escalate or invest more coverage
- They say a renewal is "safe" — qualify it out loud

**Reach for TARO when:**
- Diagnosis and qualification are done and they need a next action
- Building a repeatable play from a recurring pattern
- They know what is wrong and need a motion
- Designing a program rather than handling a single account

---

## How they interlock

SPICED's **Pain** and **Impact** (Winning by Design) overlap with MEDDPICC's
**Implicate the Pain** and **Metrics** (MEDDICC) — similar evidence, used first
to understand and then to qualify. Keep each framework's own term.

SPICED's **Decision** covers ground that MEDDPICC splits into **Decision
Criteria**, **Decision Process**, and **Paper Process**. Use SPICED's D for a
quick read; use MEDDPICC's three when real money is on the line.

MEDDPICC gaps become TARO **Triggers**. "Economic Buyer unknown, renewal in 80
days" is a trigger. The TARO play is the motion that closes the gap, and its
Outcome is the gap being closed.

A **Critical Event** (SPICED) and a **Decision Process** (MEDDPICC) together
produce a credible date. Either one alone does not.

---

## Sequencing by lifecycle stage

- **Stage 0–1 (Handoff, Onboarding)** — SPICED to validate what Sales captured.
  Handoff quality scoring checks whether the SPICED survived the transition.
- **Stage 2–3 (Adoption, Nurture)** — SPICED at each QBR. TARO for health-signal
  plays.
- **Stage 4 (Expansion)** — SPICED to find the new use case, MEDDPICC to qualify
  it before calling it pipeline.
- **Stage 5 (Renewal)** — MEDDPICC as the primary lens, re-run SPICED if anything
  material changed.
- **Stage 7 (Churn)** — post-mortem against both. Which element was unknown, and
  when did it become unknowable?

---

## Anti-patterns

**Framework stacking.** Running all three on a simple question. If someone asks
whether to send a follow-up email, answer the question.

**Qualifying before diagnosing.** MEDDPICC on an account whose Situation you
cannot describe produces confident answers to the wrong questions.

**Diagnosing forever.** SPICED is not the deliverable. At some point it becomes a
play.

**Framework as performance.** Naming the letters is not the value. A CS leader
does not need to hear "let's run MEDDPICC" — they need to hear "who signs this,
and have you met them?" Use the structure to organize your thinking, and speak in
plain language.
