# The Bowtie Model

## What it is

Winning by Design's lifecycle model for recurring revenue businesses. It
connects acquisition, retention and expansion into one system, replacing the
funnel, which stops at the sale.

## Stages

**Acquisition (left side)**
- **Awareness** — the customer recognizes a problem and discovers a solution
  exists
- **Education** — explores the problem and possible solutions, deepening their
  understanding of their needs
- **Selection** — compares options, validates fit, decides

**Commit (the knot)** — the prospect becomes a customer and delivery begins.

**Retention and expansion (right side)**
- **Onboard** — activated, trained and guided toward initial outcomes
- **Impact** — experiences measurable value and results
- **Expansion** — grows through more usage, additional products, or renewal

## Metric types (from the Bowtie Standard summary)

- **Volume** — how many are in each stage
- **Conversion** — movement between stages
- **Velocity** — time taken to move through stages

These connect end to end across the lifecycle.

## Principles

- Growth requires acquisition **and** retention **and** expansion.
- **Impact, not promised value, drives renewal** and long-term revenue.
- Teams need a shared language and standard model.
- Go-to-market motions should be managed like systems, with closed-loop feedback.

## Problems it is meant to solve

- Teams optimizing their own stage instead of the whole lifecycle
- No clear way to diagnose where growth is breaking
- Missed expansion
- Siloed sales and customer success

## Account-management relevance (model note)

The right side of the Bowtie is the AM's territory. The central claim for the
coach: renewal and expansion follow **Impact**, so an account that has not reached
Impact is not ready for an expansion motion.
