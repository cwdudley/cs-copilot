# Customer Success Metrics — Harvesting vs. Mining

## Two ways to get value evidence

**Harvesting (proactive)**
- Sales defines success metrics (M2s), grounded in proven outcomes (M1s), during
  the sale
- Baselines are measured and tracked through implementation
- The delivery team inherits a clear definition of success
- Realized value (M3s) feeds back into the M1 library

**Mining (reactive)**
- Needed when continuity broke — usually a weak sales-to-delivery handoff
- Requires investigative work to connect functional improvements to business
  results
- Takes much more effort than harvesting
- Often triggered by lost baseline documentation or executives drifting out of
  alignment

## Why customers can't produce metrics on their own

1. **Short memory** — solved problems are forgotten quickly
2. **Attribution is hard** — good outcomes rarely have a single cause
3. **No focus** — metrics have to be deliberately cultivated and collected

## Resetting a drifted relationship

**Diagnostic questions:**
- Why did the customer originally engage?
- Which of their original goals have changed?
- What value has been delivered, and who knows about it?
- Where were opportunities missed?

**Common mistake:** assuming the original Champion still exists. A reset means
finding new pain, getting stakeholder access, developing a new Champion, and
re-establishing executive alignment.

## Examples

- **Clear attribution:** a CRM data cleanup saved a customer about $30,000 in
  media costs in the first year on a $10,000 investment.
- **Shared attribution:** a supply-chain solution contributed to a 20% improvement
  in on-time-in-full delivery and a 10% satisfaction increase, alongside other
  factors.

## Mining process when there is no baseline

1. Understand what was supposed to be delivered
2. Verify it was implemented and adopted properly
3. Measure impact up the chain: functional improvement → operational outcome →
   business result

## Failure modes behind mining

Disrupted handovers, undocumented baselines, executive alignment drift, personnel
changes, and weak execution across the MEDDPICC elements during the sale.

## Relevance to renewal and expansion

Dunkel's view: repairing an existing customer relationship is easier than landing
new business, and a restored alignment is where new metrics and expansion
opportunities come from.
