# The SaaS Sales Method

## The problem with the funnel

The traditional sales funnel ends at the initial purchase, which ignores the
72–93% of customer lifetime value Winning by Design attributes to renewals and
expansion after the sale.

## The Bowtie

- **Left side (acquisition):** Awareness → Education → Selection
- **Knot:** the commit / close
- **Right side (retention and growth):** Onboarding → First impact → Renewal →
  Expansion

## The method

An approach spanning Marketing, Sales and Customer Success, centered on
**discovering, communicating and delivering business impact consistently across
the whole customer journey**.

The key shift: from **qualification-driven** selling (a one-time event) to
**impact-driven** selling (continuous for the life of the relationship).

## Seven shifts

| From | To | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Fit | **Business results** | Sell the outcome delivered, not product fit |
| Qualification | **Conversation** | Natural dialogue over interrogation |
| Pitching | **Diagnosing** | Understand before recommending |
| Negotiating | **Trading** | Exchange equal value instead of mutual concession |
| Onboarding | **Orchestrating** | Guide the whole business relationship |
| Usage | **Results** | Measure outcomes achieved, not product utilization |
| Upselling | **Growing** | Develop the relationship rather than "land and expand" |

## Relationship to classic methodologies

- **Solution selling** — customer knows the problem; seller helps them choose
- **Consultative selling** — customer knows the problem; seller educates on
  approaches
- **Strategic selling** — seller identifies problems the customer has not
  recognized, including executive engagement

Winning by Design describes its method as combining elements of all three.

## Account-management relevance

Three of the seven shifts are post-sale and define the AM stance directly:
**orchestrating, results, growing**. Expansion is framed as growing the
relationship through delivered impact, not upselling.
