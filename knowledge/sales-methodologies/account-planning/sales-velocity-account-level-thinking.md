# Beyond Opportunity Count — The Sales Velocity Levers

## Core argument

Revenue growth is not mainly a function of adding more opportunities. The
**sales velocity equation**:

> Sales Velocity = (Average Contract Value × Conversion Rate × Number of
> Opportunities) ÷ Time to Close

Modest gains on all four levers compound to more than a large gain on
opportunity count alone.

## The four levers

1. **Average contract value** — deal size
2. **Conversion rate** — win rate
3. **Number of opportunities** — pipeline volume
4. **Time to close** — cycle length

## Eight practices

| Practice | Lever it moves |
|---|---|
| Qualify by asking whether you are valuable to the customer, not whether there is a deal for you | Conversion, opportunity quality |
| Prepare deeply — it signals care and builds trust | ACV, pain discovery |
| Understand the Decision Process early | Time to close |
| Engage multiple departments to implicate wider business impact | ACV |
| Map decision-makers, their priorities and their pain, then differentiate | Conversion |
| Educate customers on technical, economic and relationship criteria | Conversion |
| Tie value to time-bound business goals with measurable outcomes | Time to close |
| Find a Compelling Event — without one, selling becomes order-taking | Time to close, conversion |

## Account-management relevance

For an AM, "more opportunities" is the least efficient lever. Expanding value
across departments and building multiple Champion relationships moves contract
value and retention together.
