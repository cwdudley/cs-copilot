# Target Account Lists and Tiering

> Written for new-logo account-based strategies. Included because it was the
> only public Winning by Design account-prioritization material found during
> retrieval. It does not address existing customers.

## Start from your best customers

Build the ideal customer profile from customers with the **largest contracts,
contract expansions, most referrals**, or (in SaaS) the most active usage.

**Look for what they have in common:**
- Industry or vertical
- Geography
- Company size — revenue and budget
- Headcount
- Their own customer base
- Company maturity
- Organization type — public/private, for-profit/nonprofit

## Data types

- **Firmographic** — industry, location, customer count, revenue
- **Technographic** — the technologies they use
- **In-market signals** — real-time indicators of buying intent

## Three tiers

| Tier | Definition | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Close match to your best customers | Highest |
| **Tier 2** | Meets most criteria with minor deviations | Moderate |
| **Tier 3** | Longer shots | Lighter touch |

Example allocation from the article: of 500 qualified accounts, 60 in Tier 1, 100
in Tier 2 and 240 in Tier 3.

## Measuring it

Track engagement, growth in average contract value, and cycle length. Refresh no
more than about 20% of the list per quarter so accounts have time to respond.

## Transfer to an existing book (model note)

The article does not discuss existing customers; its only related mention is
reactivating contacts who moved to other companies. Applying the tiering logic to
an existing book is this project's inference: tier by fit, plus inputs new-logo
lists cannot have, such as achieved impact, account health and usage.
