# Expansion Types, Ownership, and the Post-Sale Bowtie

## Post-sale sub-systems

- **Onboarding** (called Activation in product-led models) — get the customer to
  their initial impact as fast as possible, whether that takes weeks of
  integration or seconds.
- **Adoption** — the customer builds the product into daily routines; the
  provider supplies training, support and updates. Effective adoption leads
  naturally to renewal.
- **Expansion** — growing the business with the customer: more licenses, higher
  tiers, additional modules and features.

## Value vs. Impact

- **Value** is a promise of future impact — the benefits a customer expects when
  buying.
- **Impact** is the fulfillment of that promise — measurable results in actual
  use.

Ownership (pay-upfront) models sell Value. Subscription and consumption models
depend on delivering **continuous, measurable Impact**, because the customer keeps
paying only if impact keeps happening. Organizations therefore have to shift
their attention to the right side of the Bowtie.

## Operationalizing it backward

Work upstream from what the customer is trying to achieve:

1. Is the customer actually **adopting** the product? If not, fix that.
2. Did they reach **first Impact** in the first place?
3. What **Impact did we commit at the close, and by when?**

Mapping backward from the customer's goal aligns every team's resources.

## Post-sale conversion points (as numbered in this document)

| Point | Measures |
|---|---|
| Onboarding | Share of the committed cohort successfully onboarded (a retention figure) |
| Retention | Gross revenue retention — recurring revenue kept, including downgrades and cancellations |
| Expansion | New ARR from upsells and expansions in the first year, as a percentage of the cohort's post-churn ARR |

Other Winning by Design papers number these conversion rates differently; the
definitions, not the numbers, are what carry across.

## The four forms of expansion

Expansion grows lifetime value, and when done well it costs far less than
acquisition.

| Form | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Upsell** | Selling additional products, features or higher tiers to existing customers — add-ons, premium features, higher usage limits, more advanced capability. Can include geographic expansion or annual price increases. |
| **Cross-sell** | Selling complementary products or services to a **different set of stakeholders** in an existing customer — other teams, departments or business units. The document's example: separate business groups inside one parent company. |
| **Renewal and extension** | Renewing and extending the subscription, sometimes adjusting terms, duration or scope. Renewals often happen automatically at or just before the contract anniversary. Drivers: recurring impact, a smooth renewal process, a good user experience, and customer satisfaction — especially how fast issues are resolved. |
| **Resell** | Required when the current Champion, alpha user or decision-maker leaves their role. The replacement may be tasked with cutting costs and has no emotional connection to the product, so they must be resold immediately. |

## Growth areas: impact × benefactor

Two variables drive growth inside an account: **the impact** of the solution, and
**the benefactor** — the person or team who benefits from that impact.

| Situation | Motion | Ownership guidance from the document |
|---|---|---|
| An existing, excited benefactor increases impact — more seats, a premium tier, new products | **Upsell** | A CSM can handle it reactively; proactive outreach generally needs an acquisition-oriented Account Manager |
| A **new** benefactor inside an existing account must be won over | **Cross-sell** | Should **never** be handled by a CSM — it is the most complicated sale, and may require displacing a competitor another Champion chose |
| The Champion leaves their position, including through promotion | **Resell** | Resell the new person immediately or churn risk rises at once |

**Resell is two-sided.** If the Champion leaves for another company, it is also a
new-deal opportunity, and the departing Champion can become an advocate there.

## Closed loops

Growth compounds through loops on the right side of the Bowtie:

1. **Renewals** — renewing and expanding compounds growth.
2. **Risk sharing** — during onboarding, customers often mention another team that
   could benefit, which spreads their own risk. The document suggests building
   that question into the onboarding process.
3. **Advocacy** — customers promoting you publicly or through advisory boards.
4. **Ideal customer profile** — refining acquisition from your best customers.
5. **Referrals** — customers discussing you with peers, even mid-sale.
6. **Nurturing** — interested non-buyers kept informed.

Customers often mention you to peers **before** reaching their desired impact,
because nobody wants to be the only one among their peers who chose you.

The unifying thread across all customer-facing roles is **Impact**; closed loops
only work when roles share that goal instead of operating in silos.
