# Trade Instead of Negotiate

## Trading vs. negotiating

**Negotiation** is adversarial: both sides haggle to a compromise and often both
feel they lost something.

**Trading** puts both parties on the same side of the table. Each gives what it
has in abundance in exchange for what it needs.

## Why discounting fails

- Dropping price after quoting it tells the customer the original price was not
  real.
- In recurring revenue, a discount compounds. The article's illustration: a 10%
  discount, carried and compounded over seven years, leaves revenue at roughly
  half of what full price would have produced.

**Reframe a discount as a price adjustment**, which sets the expectation that the
customer gives something back.

## Trading levers

The article names three core levers — price, contract length and payment terms —
and separately lists additional assets that should not be overlooked.

| Lever | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Price** | Unpack the objection first — is it total contract value or the upfront payment? |
| **Contract length** | Month-to-month vs. one- or two-year terms |
| **Payment terms** | Net 30/45/60; annual prepay vs. monthly |
| **Additional assets** | Case study, testimonial, executive quote, written review, or an expansion workshop inside their organization |

**Keep all levers on the table together.** Experienced buyers try to isolate each
lever and negotiate them one at a time.

## Preparing for a trade meeting

- Review the overall strategy and align internal colleagues
- List the trade requests you expect
- Research the attendees
- Pre-meet your internal champion to understand stakeholder concerns

## Running the trade

1. **Make the proposal** — summarize price, payment terms, contract length and
   implementation fees together
2. **Invite questions** — listen without emotion; play back what you heard
3. **Prioritize** — ask the customer which issue matters most
4. **Qualify the decision-makers** — confirm the people present can commit
5. **Start trading** — begin with the easiest items; never give without getting
   something back. A late extra demand is met with: that changes the deal.
6. **Hear counteroffers** — repeat them back; have them rank multiple requests
7. **Ask for commitment** — directly ask whether they are ready to move forward
   together
8. **Set an expiration** — with a stated consequence, such as reverting to the
   original terms
9. **Confirm in writing** — an email within about 30 minutes summarizing the terms
   and the expiration date

## Implied mistakes (model note)

Jumping to a price cut before understanding the objection; letting the buyer
split the levers; reacting emotionally to hard asks; and not confirming agreements
in writing with an expiry.

## Account-management relevance (model note)

Directly applicable to renewals and expansions, where the "additional assets" category — references, case studies, an expansion workshop — is often more valuable
to both sides than price.
