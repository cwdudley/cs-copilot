# Closing Deals Without Being in the Room

## Buying committees have grown

The episode cites committees growing from around 7 stakeholders to 14 or more in
enterprise deals — as many as 22 when boards are involved. One Champion can no
longer cover every decision-maker.

## Multiple Champions

Build Champions across functions. People leave and restructures happen, and
different Champions carry weight in different parts of the organization.

## Champion decks

Build the deck your Champion will present internally, in the customer's own
branding. Doing so forces you to think from the buyer's side.

## A Champion test

Deliberately change a metric in the material and ask the Champion to review it.
If they catch it, they are engaged with the substance. If they do not, their
buy-in is in question.

## Overlooked Champions

**Procurement** and **cloud marketplace** contacts are often missed. They can
unlock budget routes or approval authority that the business unit does not know
exist.

## The new business meeting

Not a demo handoff. Use it to identify potential Champions, uncover pain at
multiple levels, and build a Champion plan before discussing the solution.

## Coaching questions this supports

- How many Champions do you have, and in which functions?
- If your main Champion left tomorrow, who carries the deal?
- Has anyone in procurement been treated as a potential ally?
