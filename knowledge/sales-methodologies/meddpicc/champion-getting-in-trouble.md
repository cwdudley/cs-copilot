# Getting in Trouble With Your Champion

## Initial resistance is normal

Shared interests do not mean instant agreement. A real Champion will come around;
the seller's job is to work through the early pushback rather than retreat from
it.

## Find the Champion's line

Every Champion has a limit to how far they will advocate internally. Most sellers
never get near it. Deliberately testing that boundary is part of building a real
Champion relationship.

## Good sellers vs. nice sellers

- **Nice sellers** do what is comfortable and avoid friction.
- **Good sellers** do what is right for the outcome, even when it is
  uncomfortable.

The difference shows up in results.

## Lend your buying expertise

You are an expert in selling; your Champion is not an expert in buying. Use
stories from past deals to give them credibility and bring them along.

## Core advice

Sometimes you have to risk annoying your Champion — as long as the direction
genuinely serves both sides' success.

## Coaching questions this supports (model note)

- What have you asked of your Champion that they initially resisted?
- Where is their line, and have you tested it?
- Are you avoiding an ask because it is uncomfortable?
