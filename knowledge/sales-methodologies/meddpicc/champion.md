# Champion

## Definition

A person with power, influence and credibility inside the customer who is
willing and able to help you make progress.

**No Champion, no deal.** MEDDICC also uses the companion line: big Champion,
big deal. Even with every other element strong, a deal without a Champion is
missing its most important asset.

## The three required traits

| Trait | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Power and influence** | Organizational standing to move decisions among stakeholders |
| **Sells internally for you** | Advocates for the solution when you are not in the room |
| **Vested interest** | A genuine personal or departmental stake in the outcome |

All three are required. A friendly primary point of contact is not
automatically a Champion.

## Champion vs. Coach vs. Friend

| Role | Characteristics | Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Coach** | Gives useful insight and introductions, but lacks power/influence or vested interest | Can be developed into a Champion if they gain influence or a stake |
| **Champion** | Meets all three traits; drives the deal internally | Keep testing throughout the cycle |
| **Friend** | Risk zone: the seller becomes reluctant to hold them accountable; the relationship turns emotional instead of outcome-driven | Refocus on mutual benefit; keep professional distance |

## Building a Champion

- **Trust** — built through consistency, reliability and honesty. Champions who
  trust you work harder and share more.
- **Not friendship** — the relationship should be outcome-driven, with both
  sides benefiting from each other's success.
- **Equip them** — they must understand the solution well enough to defend it.
  Use the Three Whys (why anything, why you, why now) so they can make the case
  to others.
- **Test continuously** — re-qualify throughout the process.
- **Remember competitors have Champions too** — the stronger Champion wins.

## Practical check

Ask regularly: does this person meet all three traits? If not, developing a real
Champion is the immediate priority.
