# SPICED vs. MEDDPICC — MEDDICC's View

> **Source bias:** this is the MEDDPICC vendor's comparison and favors
> MEDDPICC. Winning by Design's counterpart is preserved separately in
> `spiced/comparison-meddic-and-spiced-wbd-view.md`.

## How MEDDICC describes SPICED

- **Situation** — the buyer's current setup, team, tools
- **Pain** — the gap between current and desired state
- **Impact** — business consequences: revenue, time, resources, risk
- **Critical Event** — the deadline or trigger creating urgency
- **Decision** — process, stakeholders, evaluation criteria

## Element mapping (MEDDICC's framing)

| SPICED | Closest MEDDPICC | MEDDICC's distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | Economic Buyer, Decision Process | MEDDPICC maps stakeholders explicitly; SPICED treats them as context |
| Pain | Implicate the Pain | MEDDPICC develops pain throughout the deal |
| Impact | Metrics | MEDDPICC structures quantification (economic, efficiency, risk) |
| Critical Event | Decision Process | MEDDPICC embeds urgency in decision timing |
| Decision | Criteria, Process, EB, Champion, Competition | MEDDPICC separates what SPICED holds as one element |
| — | Champion | No SPICED equivalent |
| — | Competition | No SPICED equivalent |

## Strengths MEDDICC credits to SPICED

- More consultative and buyer-centric than BANT
- Critical Event forces specificity on urgency
- Applies across the full lifecycle, acquisition through renewal
- Built for recurring-revenue models

## Gaps MEDDICC claims in SPICED

- **Stakeholders** — no Champion concept
- **Post-decision process** — no Paper Process equivalent
- **Value quantification** — Impact lacks a systematic structure
- **Competitive risk** — no mapping of alternatives, including build and status quo

## MEDDICC's conclusion

SPICED suits mid-market SaaS; MEDDPICC scales with more rigor for complex
enterprise deals. The article does not discuss using the two together.
