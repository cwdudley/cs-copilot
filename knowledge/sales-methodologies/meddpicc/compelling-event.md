# The Compelling Event in MEDDPICC

## Definition

The specific, time-bound reason a buyer must act: a business, financial or
regulatory consequence where doing nothing costs more than acting.

## Not a separate letter

MEDDICC deliberately does not add a letter for it. A Compelling Event runs
through Pain, Metrics, Champion, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Competition
and Decision Process.

**Why not a third C?** Paper Process earned a letter because sellers genuinely
forgot to address it. A missing Compelling Event is a **skill gap** — the
opportunity was not properly qualified across the existing elements. A new field
would not build the skill.

## How it shows up in each element

| Element | Role of the Compelling Event |
|---|---|
| Implicate the Pain | Pain drives change only when it is compelling in its own right |
| Metrics | Metrics compel action when quantified against a specific timeframe |
| Champion | Needs something time-bound to justify the personal risk of championing you |
| Economic Buyer | Prioritizes the Champion's initiative only when there is a compelling reason to act |
| Decision Criteria | Linking your strengths to the pain makes your position defensible |
| Decision Process | Progress depends on an energized Champion with EB sponsorship — which depends on urgency |

## Real vs. manufactured

**Real:**
- Compliance deadline or audit date
- Contract renewal or automatic price uplift with the incumbent
- Board-mandated cost or headcount target with a fixed date
- A competitor already live in the account

**Manufactured:**
- A limited-time discount (it resets with every delay)
- A decision date the seller picked
- General pressure to "move fast" with no named consequence

**The test:** a real Compelling Event exists whether or not you are in the room.
A manufactured one disappears the moment you stop pushing it.

## Discovery method

1. Find a specific date tied to a business, financial or regulatory consequence.
2. Confirm that **both** the Champion and the Economic Buyer can state the cost
   of missing it.

If neither can name the consequence of inaction, you have found a **preference**,
not a Compelling Event.

## Common mistakes

- Treating it as a checkbox rather than a principle
- Creating artificial urgency (discounts) instead of uncovering real time pressure
- Arriving too late in the buyer's journey to shape urgency
- Not attaching quantified metrics to the timeframe
