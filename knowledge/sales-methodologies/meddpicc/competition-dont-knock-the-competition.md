# Why You Shouldn't Knock the Competition

## Three problems with attacking rivals

1. **It signals insecurity** — you look defensive, not confident.
2. **It damages credibility** — if the customer disagrees, you lose trust.
3. **It wastes scarce time** — airtime spent on a competitor's weaknesses is not
   spent on your strengths.

Whyte describes a public attack by one tech CEO on a struggling competitor that
backfired: experts rebutted the claims, customers questioned the attacker's
integrity, and neutral observers sided with the target.

## Build your building taller

Out-compete instead of tearing down:

- Be exceptionally prepared and researched
- Uncover and implicate the customer's pain before anyone else
- Build a comprehensive business case
- Influence the decision criteria toward your strengths
- Develop a stronger Champion than the competition
- Shape and follow a clear decision process

## The most effective tactic

Whyte's view: nothing beats influencing the **Decision Criteria** toward your
strengths. It outperforms fear, uncertainty and doubt.

## Account-management relevance (model note)

At renewal, a competitor returning to the account is best answered by
documented realized value and criteria the customer already agreed matter — not
by criticizing the alternative.
