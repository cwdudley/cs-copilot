# Obsessing Over the Wrong Competition

## The real field

Sellers fixate on rival vendors. The actual competitive set also includes
self-build and competing internal initiatives — and the largest, least visible
competitor: **inertia**, where the customer engages with you and then keeps the
status quo. These are the deals that slip quarter after quarter.

## Why deals slip to inertia — three misqualification areas

**1. Value**
The pain is not acute enough. The customer has not been implicated in it, and
risk-averse organizations do not self-motivate change.

**2. Stakeholders**
No Champion. Without internal advocacy, momentum stalls regardless of how real
the pain is.

**3. Process**
Unmapped dependencies. The article's example: a deal required the customer to
complete a separate transformation step first. When that dependency was ignored,
the deal lost momentum. Legal and procurement involvement can add competing
departmental interests.

## Coaching questions this supports (model note)

- If this deal slips, will it be to a vendor or to nothing?
- Which of value, stakeholders or process is weakest?
- What has to happen on the customer's side before this can proceed?
