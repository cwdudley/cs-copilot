# Lessons From Implementing MEDDICC — Andy Whyte

## 1. It is all or nothing

Whyte describes MEDDICC as binary: used fully or not at all. SDRs, marketing,
sales operations and leadership need the same terminology, embedded in CRM and
review processes.

## 2. Front-line managers decide adoption

The biggest success factor is front-line manager buy-in. Whyte suggests spending
about two hours training managers for every hour spent training sellers.

## 3. Executive sponsorship

Leaders who understand MEDDICC get more predictable forecasts. Executives new to
it need a deliberate buy-in plan or the initiative gets deprioritized.

## 4. Celebrate wins publicly

Highlight moments where it clicks — qualifying out, finding a real Champion,
agreeing decision criteria.

## 5. Coach continuously

One-time training does not stick. If you feel like you are repeating yourself,
you probably have not repeated it enough.

## 6. Mandatory weekly deal reviews

Structured weekly reviews through the MEDDICC lens accelerate adoption and peer
learning.

## 7. Confidence scoring with defined criteria

Score each element from 1 to 10 for confidence. This exposes pipeline health —
but only with written definitions of what each score means, to counter optimism
bias.

## 8. Partner with marketing

Marketing can support stakeholder-specific programs, such as Champion-building
events and Economic Buyer roundtables, and persona-based content.

## 9. Combine with value selling

MEDDICC qualifies the opportunity; value selling turns what you learned into
customer outcomes. They complement each other.

## 10. Build it into onboarding

New hires need formal training and certification, including experienced people,
because each organization applies the framework differently.

## Reported results

Whyte reports growing one team from 6 to 20 people with more than 100% revenue
growth, and in another region doubling average order value while cutting cycle
times by about 30%.
