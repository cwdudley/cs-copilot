# MEDDPICC Deal Reviews

## Forward-looking, not a post-mortem

Deal reviews are often treated as retrospective autopsies of what already
happened. MEDDICC argues they should be proactive strategy sessions: anticipate
obstacles, plan responses, and chart the next moves — thinking several moves
ahead, like chess.

Pim Roelofsen calls deal reviews the most powerful cadence a team can commit to,
because they close the gap between knowing the methodology and applying it.

## The 100-hour rule

MEDDICC estimates mastery takes around 100 hours of deliberate practice. A weekly
one-hour deal review across roughly 44 working weeks yields about 44 hours a year
— more than 40% of the way.

## Cadence and integration

Run deal reviews on a consistent weekly rhythm, and connect them with:

- One-on-one coaching
- Quarterly business reviews
- Win/loss analysis
- Forecast calls

Repetition builds a flywheel: each review reinforces the shared language until
applying MEDDPICC becomes instinct rather than a checklist.

## Psychological safety

Reviews only work if people can openly raise weaknesses — for example, no access
to the Economic Buyer. Leaders are responsible for keeping that environment. A
review that punishes gaps teaches people to hide them.

## Leadership sets the tone

Active participation by leaders signals that the practice matters. Consistent
reviews build a culture of introspection, accountability and shared learning.

## Customize it

The format can and should be adapted to the organization and industry.
