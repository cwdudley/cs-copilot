# Proactive Decision Criteria — Lessons From MEDDICC's Own Buying

Whyte and Roelofsen use their own experience as buyers to show what happens when
vendors wait for criteria to form instead of shaping them.

## The Economic Buyer lens: three Cs

- **Cost** — including time and resources, not only money
- **Completion** — likelihood the implementation actually succeeds
- **Confidence** — stakeholder conviction in the choice

Price mattered, but it was not the only factor; strategic alignment weighed as
heavily.

## Mistakes the vendors made

1. **Relying on the Champion alone.** One vendor leaned on a single internal
   advocate and never connected its solution to the pain of another key
   influencer, who stayed uninformed.
2. **No differentiation tied to business goals.** Neither vendor linked its
   offering to the buyer's retention objective, so the decision came down to
   price.
3. **Executive outreach without groundwork.** A vendor CEO contacted MEDDICC's
   CEO without first preparing with the actual Champion, which created
   awkwardness despite good intent.
4. **Referencing a competitor negatively.**

## What to do instead

1. Discover each stakeholder's priorities properly.
2. Tie the solution to each influencer's specific pain.
3. Coordinate with the Champion before any executive outreach.
4. Leave competitors out of the conversation.

## Coaching questions this supports

- If this came down to price today, what criterion would you have failed to shape?
- Which influencer has not heard how this addresses their pain?
- Has the Champion been briefed before your executive reaches out?
