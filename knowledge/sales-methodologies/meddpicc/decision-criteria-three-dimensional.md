# Three-Dimensional Decision Criteria

## The dimensions in more depth

**Technical** — features and functionality. Necessary, but competing only here
leaves most differentiation unused.

**Economic** — more than list price: CapEx vs. OpEx treatment, contract length,
ROI, and total cost of ownership.

**Relationship** — whether the customer believes the working relationship will
be good and trusts your organization. Built from reputation and referrals.
Intangible, frequently overlooked, and capable of deciding a deal.

## Example of proactive differentiation

Two security vendors are functionally near-identical. One proactively points out
that it can be bought through the customer's existing cloud marketplace. That
single point addresses an **economic** criterion (easier procurement, committed
spend) and a **relationship** criterion (aligned incentives) at once.

## Qualification use

Identify which criteria are non-negotiable for this customer. If you cannot meet
one, either qualify out early or explore a different delivery or partner model.

## Retrospective use

Review past wins and ask which criteria actually decided them. That tells you
where to focus in current opportunities.

## Scope

The three dimensions apply across the whole customer lifecycle, not only new
sales.
