# Decision Criteria

## Definition

The criteria by which the decision to buy will be judged — the principles,
guidelines and requirements the organization uses to evaluate options.

## Three types

| Type | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Technical** | Does the solution meet the functional and technical requirements? |
| **Economic** | Is it financially viable — cost, risk, operational efficiency? |
| **Relationship** | Do the two organizations' values and strategic direction align? |

Weighting varies by role: a CTO leans technical, a CFO leans economic.

## Why it matters

Without defined criteria, customers default to gut feel, and the seller is
working blind.

## Customers often lack criteria

Many customers have not defined their criteria up front. MEDDICC places
responsibility on the seller to educate the customer on what they should
prioritize.

## Influencing criteria

Shape criteria toward your genuine differentiated strengths. Criteria that only
you can meet set a trap competitors cannot get out of. Demonstrate the capability
and why it matters to the business.

Criteria are not fixed. Assuming requirements are locked limits effectiveness.

## Connections

- **Metrics** quantify why a criterion matters.
- **Competition** is evaluated against the same criteria.
- **Economic Buyer** weighs the economic criteria most heavily.
