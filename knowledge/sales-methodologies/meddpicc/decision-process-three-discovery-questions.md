# Three Discovery Questions That Compress the Cycle

## 1. Look backward at the last purchase

Ask what happened the last time they bought a comparable solution.

**Why it works:** recalling real past steps is more accurate than projecting a
hypothetical future. It surfaces the actual Decision Process — who was involved,
in what order — and exposes obstacles likely to recur.

**Uncovers:** Decision Process, Paper Process, hidden approvers.

## 2. Offer a pattern, then ask for theirs

Share what you typically see at organizations like theirs — a quantified pain
such as a typical churn rate — then ask what it looks like for them.

**Why it works:** it shows credibility earned with other customers and invites
them to compare against their own numbers, which surfaces Metrics even when the
customer is initially reluctant to share.

**Uncovers:** Metrics, Pain.

## 3. Reflect their next steps back

Observe that it sounds like they already have next steps in mind (a technique
MEDDICC attributes to negotiator Chris Voss).

**Why it works:** it lets the customer propose the path forward without feeling
pressured, making them more open about timelines and progression.

**Uncovers:** Decision Process, timing, commitment.

## Account-management adaptation

Question 1 transfers directly to renewals and expansions: how did the last
renewal or add-on purchase actually get approved, by whom, and how long did
procurement take?
