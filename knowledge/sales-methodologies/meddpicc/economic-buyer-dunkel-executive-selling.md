# Economic Buyer Selling — Dick Dunkel

## What executives care about

Executives do not care about your product; they care about what it does for
their objectives and strategic initiatives.

## What an Economic Buyer can do

- Override or veto recommendations
- Reallocate budget toward or away from a project
- Direct key decision-makers
- Accelerate the cycle
- Increase deal size
- Refer you to other executives

## Five mistakes

1. **Incomplete stakeholder identification** — missing influential senior
   leaders beyond the obvious decision-makers
2. **Insufficient strategic research** — not knowing their priorities and
   initiatives
3. **Poor executive communication** — messaging not in executive language and KPIs
4. **Single-channel outreach** — relying on one route in
5. **Funding blindness** — not uncovering how budget and capital are allocated

## Research before engaging

- Annual reports / 10-K filings and earnings announcements for stated initiatives
- How executives are compensated
- Which executives are tied to the relevant initiatives
- Know names and titles before you ask for access

## Value Pyramid (hypothesis before discovery)

- **Top:** corporate objectives (e.g., shareholder value)
- **Business strategy** (e.g., customer growth)
- **Initiatives:** specific goals with timelines
- **Foundation:** execution challenges and required capabilities

Build this as a hypothesis before discovery, then test it.

## Getting engagement

Executives respond when you:

1. Establish credibility (mutual contacts, relevant experience)
2. Show business acumen about their industry context
3. Offer proof points: quantified outcomes and comparable examples
4. Ask for a short introduction, including via their assistant

Dunkel reports executives are about three times more likely to engage through
multi-channel outreach: your own contact, internal leadership introductions,
executive assistants, partners, existing customers, events.

## Funding discovery questions

- Is there a defined, allocated budget?
- How are funds secured for unplanned investments?
- Who approves an increase in spend for a higher-return opportunity?

## Executive alignment

Dunkel recommends an explicit executive-alignment phase before committing major
effort, to confirm the opportunity is worth pursuing.

## Context

Executives currently face tighter budgets, more purchase scrutiny, delayed
decisions and lower delegated authority. They are benchmarked against peers and
rely on trusted advisors for business insight, not product information.
