# Qualifying the Economic Buyer

## Budget Holder is not the Economic Buyer

A **Budget Holder** manages a department's spend. An **Economic Buyer** deploys
budget to achieve organizational outcomes. Confusing the two is a common
qualification error.

## Identification signals

- Authority to veto regardless of Champion support; can start or stop initiatives
- Alignment with quarterly and annual strategic planning
- Access to funds beyond normal departmental budgets
- P&L accountability
- Involvement in contractual approval and signature authority

## Two opposite qualification mistakes

1. **Under-qualification** — stopping the search too early, often by accepting a
   Budget Holder as the Economic Buyer.
2. **Over-qualification** — assuming the CEO must be the Economic Buyer. In
   practice this is rarely the case.

## Discovery approach

Use a disciplined, process-driven approach that still leaves room for instinct.
Investigate organizational dynamics, how budgets are really set, and role
definitions — none of which are visible from outside. Do not anchor
qualification on budget roles and budget processes alone.

## What Economic Buyers evaluate

- Cost
- Time to value
- Ease of adoption for end users

They weigh organizational impact over features.

## Post-signature

Keep the Economic Buyer updated on implementation outcomes after the deal. This
strengthens future upsell positioning and makes them a credible reference.

## Tactic

Bring a sales leader from your side into Economic Buyer meetings to signal
seniority and the ability to commit.
