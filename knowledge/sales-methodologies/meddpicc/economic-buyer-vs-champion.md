# Mistaking the Champion for the Economic Buyer

## The confusion

Sellers often treat their Champion — the internal advocate — as the Economic
Buyer. The roles do different work:

- **Champion:** pulls people together, works procurement, chases responses,
  advocates internally.
- **Economic Buyer:** holds overall buying authority and can cut through friction
  in procurement and negotiation.

## Why it matters even when you win

Whyte frames success not as win/loss but as winning **for the most value, as
quickly as possible**. Without the real Economic Buyer engaged:

- Procurement, legal and negotiation drag on
- Deal value erodes through margin pressure
- The long-term relationship is weaker

## When they are the same person

Occasionally, in small companies or founder-led buying, one person genuinely
holds both roles. Even then, find a delegate Champion for the day-to-day work —
an Economic Buyer does not have the bandwidth to run the deal.

## Discovery questions

- Who is going to sign this?
- What is the CFO's view?
- Who could stop this deal, and why?
- How did purchasing work last time?

## Default rule (Roelofsen)

In one deal they assumed the founder-CEO was the Economic Buyer and learned late
that the CFO held the authority. The lesson: **treat the CFO as the Economic
Buyer by default unless proven otherwise.**
