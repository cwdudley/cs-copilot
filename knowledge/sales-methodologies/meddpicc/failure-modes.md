# How MEDDPICC Fails

## Failure is usually leadership, not methodology

Whyte and Roelofsen argue that when MEDDPICC fails in a team, the framework is
rarely the problem — leadership and culture are.

### Punitive deal reviews

When managers use missing elements as a weapon, sellers stop telling the truth.
A rep criticized for not having a Champion will simply claim to have one. The
result is dishonesty that looks like compliance.

### Checkbox mentality

Teams chase turning every field green instead of finding and fixing real
weaknesses. The framework becomes camouflage rather than a lens.

### What top performers do differently

Top performers walk into a deal review **leading with their biggest gaps**, even
when uncomfortable. Weak performers bring an optimistic narrative that hides the
weaknesses. Same framework, opposite instinct.

## Three levels of use

1. **Weakest — reactive checklist / X-ray.** Reviewing deals after the fact to
   see what is missing.
2. **Stronger — common language** across go-to-market teams.
3. **Strongest — proactive.** Entering every conversation knowing which
   stakeholder, what value, and which process step you are trying to advance.

Moving up requires psychological safety around surfacing problems.

## It is not a checklist

Deals are dynamic. A new competitor can destabilize the Champion, the Decision
Criteria and the Decision Process at once. Box-ticking captures a snapshot and
leaves the seller reacting.

Top performers work proactively: co-discovering and quantifying pain, connecting
the solution to metrics, shaping criteria, and maintaining Champion
relationships.

**Champions are the clearest example.** They change priorities, change
allegiance, change roles or leave. Rather than marking "Champion: yes," strong
sellers cultivate several Champions as insurance.

## It is not one and done

Knowing what the letters stand for delivers nothing on its own. Proficiency is a
sliding scale that decays without practice. Sustained adoption means continually
applying the framework across the three domains of **Value, Stakeholders and
Process**, which also reveals new applications over time.

## Coaching questions this supports

- What is the biggest gap in this deal, stated first?
- Which element was green last month and might not be now?
- What would make you hesitant to report a gap honestly?
