# Uncovering More Metrics — The Three Ps

Customers are not buying experts. Asking vague "what are your metrics?"
questions rarely works; the seller has to structure the discovery.

## The Three Ps

**1. Preparation**
Spend roughly 20 minutes before a customer conversation researching their
business, likely concerns, common industry challenges, and where your solution
fits. That is enough to form a credible point of view.

**2. Proactivity**
Suggest likely pains instead of waiting for the customer to name them. Frame it
as pattern recognition: other customers in a similar position faced a specific
challenge — is that true here?

**3. Point of View**
Bring a perspective grounded in research and real customer data. A point of view
gives the customer something concrete to react to and correct.

## Anatomy of an M1

An M1 value story connects:

- The **pain** and its implications
- The **gain** and its implications
- The **metrics** measuring the difference between pain and gain
- The **decision criteria** needed to reach the gain
- The **stakeholders** who are aligned around it

## Coaching questions this supports

- What did you research before this call, and what hypothesis did it produce?
- Which M1 are you leading with, and why is it relevant to this customer?
- What pain did you propose, and did the customer confirm, correct or reject it?
