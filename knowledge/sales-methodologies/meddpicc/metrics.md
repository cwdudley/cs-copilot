# Metrics

## Definition

The quantifiable measures of value a solution provides. MEDDICC groups them
into three categories: **Economic**, **Efficiency**, and **Risk**.

## The M1 / M2 / M3 framework

| Type | What it is | When it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | Outcomes already delivered for existing customers, packaged as relevant use cases | Early, to earn credibility without forcing the customer to disclose numbers first |
| **M2** | Metrics personalized to this specific customer, built from real discovery | During the sales cycle, once you know what they care about |
| **M3** | M2s validated after go-live — value actually realized | Post-sale; handed to customer success; recycled into the M1 library |

The cycle is continuous: realized M3s become the M1 stories that open the next
customer conversation.

## Discovery principles

- Sharing a standard metric without learning what this customer cares about
  wastes everyone's time.
- Earn trust first; use relevant M1s to show you understand their world.
- Discovery is continuous across the cycle, not a single stage.

## Metrics create urgency — the Three Whys

Metrics support the business case that answers:

1. **Why anything?** — why change at all
2. **Why you?** — why this solution
3. **Why now?** — what is gained by acting now and lost by waiting

## Post-sale application

- M2s become M3s: the committed outcomes customer success executes against.
- Keeps implementation anchored to business outcomes, not activity.
- Gives CS an immediate understanding of the value that was promised.
- Makes renewal conversations easier because achievements are documented.

## Key mistake

Presenting metrics without understanding the customer's priorities. Test every
metric with: **who, specifically, cares about this?**
