# Analyzing Won and Lost Deals With SPICED

## Why

Surface-level loss reasons ("price," "timing") hide the real issue. Systematic
inspection of both wins and losses finds root causes, prevents repeat losses, and
reveals patterns.

## What to capture for each deal

| Element | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Situation** | How did the engagement start — inbound, outbound, referral? Who were the key contacts? |
| **Pain** | What problem prompted interest? Technology or process? What were the consequences — missed financial goals or deadlines? |
| **Impact** | What business outcome was wanted — revenue, cost reduction, customer experience? How strategically important was it? |
| **Critical Event** | What deadline applied, and what would missing it have cost? |
| **Decision** | What criteria did they use to choose? Did our team help establish those criteria? |

## Process

1. **Timing** — interview immediately after the deal closes, before the rep moves
   on to the next one
2. **Specificity** — document details; do not rely on assumptions
3. **Pattern recognition** — look for concentrations of wins or losses sharing
   characteristics
4. **Cadence** — repeat quarterly

## Example patterns the article describes

- **Wins** clustered around: newly hired champions, Critical Event dates within
  roughly 120 days, and multiple stakeholders
- **Losses** clustered around: fewer than two stakeholders, or user experience as
  the primary pain

## What to do with the findings

- Improve CRM data capture and integrity
- Qualify earlier to avoid non-viable deals
- Adjust forecast confidence and lead scoring
- Re-run the analysis each quarter

## Account-management relevance

The same inspection applies to renewals and expansions: analyze churned and
expanded accounts through SPICED to find which patterns predict each.
