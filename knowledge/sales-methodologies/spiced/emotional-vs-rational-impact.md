# Emotional vs. Rational Impact

## Definitions

| Type | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rational impact** | Measurable outcomes supported by data, usually company-level | Cost savings, revenue growth, efficiency gains |
| **Emotional impact** | Outcomes based on feelings and experience, hard to quantify, usually individual-level | Better user or employee experience, peace of mind, getting hours back each week, confidence that critical data is protected |

## Core principle

People tend to decide emotionally first and then justify the decision with facts
and figures. Emotional impact carries more weight in the decision than it usually
gets credit for.

## The traditional mistake

Conventional selling identifies needs and presents product benefits, which keeps
the conversation entirely on rational impact. It misses the emotional drivers
that turn a nice-to-have into a must-have.

## Uncovering impact

Customer needs are layered. Skilled questioning is needed to get beneath the
stated requirement to the impact the customer actually wants.

## Post-sale application

Winning by Design notes that in recurring revenue models, roughly 72–93% of
customer lifetime value comes after the initial sale. Impact therefore matters
through the whole relationship:

- **Adoption** — connect the problems being solved to measurable outcomes
- **Expansion planning** — use evidence of emotional impact to seed future growth
- **Renewal** — refer back to the impacts achieved since the start of the
  relationship

## Warning

Relying only on adoption and usage metrics reflects what the vendor needs to see,
not whether the customer succeeded — and it limits expansion.
